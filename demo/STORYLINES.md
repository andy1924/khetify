# Storylines

Three films about the same 60 seconds in Asha's day. One was built.

## A — "One continuous take" (built)

The camera never cuts. It starts inside a wheat field at 7:40 am, follows a hand as
it photographs a spotted leaf and then a fistful of soil, keeps pushing until it is
inside the phone, watches the analysis resolve into a real result screen, then keeps
pulling back — up out of the phone, over the field, into the clouds — until the single
phone is one dot in a district of dots. Price chips land on the dots. The camera
picks one dot and dives back into it, arriving at Asha's own price list. One move, one
idea: the same data at three distances.

First two seconds: a wall of wheat in the near foreground, one pale sun, and a slow
push as the field opens.

## B — "The object is the explanation"

The film is about the model. The leaf and the soil are carried into a glass box, and
the camera tours its working: image tiles, feature bands, three candidate causes. The
farmer appears only at the end, holding the phone. Strong on mechanism, weak on the
person; the app's own screens arrive late.

## C — "A number lands"

Open on a mandi board in a market yard. ₹2,520 a quintal. Then the same number in the
app, then the same number beside a farmer's field. The film argues about price only,
and the photography and soil story becomes the reason the price is trustworthy.
Sharpest for a trading audience, least true to the demo.
